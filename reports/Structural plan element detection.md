# Solve foundation plans as constraint puzzles

The best general way to detect and classify structural elements on AutoCAD-exported foundation and floor plans, without layer names, reliable colours or your own labelled data, is not a learned detector. It is a **deterministic vector pipeline that produces candidate geometry, followed by a joint, drawing-wide interpretation step**. That step assigns labels to shapes by global minimum-cost matching, with type compatibility, visibility and catalogue-consistency costs, and it enforces structural topology: footings contain columns, longrines end on supports, strips carry walls, and columns sit on the grid. A vision-language model (VLM) is optional and is used only to verify ambiguous candidates on rendered crops. No public model today can detect semelles, longrines, radiers or voiles on French structural plans. The only vector benchmark that most published models report on (FloorPlanCAD) has no column, beam, slab or footing class at all. The one dataset that does include structural classes, ArchCAD-400K, is academic-licensed, only partly released, and ships no trained checkpoint. Zero-shot VLMs and open-vocabulary detectors fail on exactly the task you need: dense, repeated, thin-line symbols with precise boxes. Your existing pipeline is therefore the right foundation. Scale calibration, style voting near labels, the type-size catalogue and uniqueness-based label recovery are all worth keeping. What must change is the decision structure. Replace per-layer, per-type greedy matching with one global assignment over all labels and all candidates. Make "style" a geometric signature rather than a layer or colour. Add independent evidence (grid, repetition, containment, parallel-band pairing, hatch detection) so that labels are one cue among several instead of the only anchor. Realistically, on clean vector PDFs, expect isolated elements (columns, isolated footings) to reach the low-to-mid 90s in recall at very high precision once the joint step and QA flags are in place. Linear and areal elements (strip footings, longrines, walls, rafts, slabs) will stay a tier lower. Raster-only sheets will stay markedly worse. Every result should carry a confidence margin, so that a human reviews the few percent of cases that are ambiguous.

## No off-the-shelf model covers French foundation elements

The learned "panoptic symbol spotting" (PSS) literature on vector CAD looks mature, but its label space excludes what you need. FloorPlanCAD, the benchmark that almost every method reports on, has 35 classes. They are dominated by doors, windows and furniture. Its only structural-ish classes are wall and curtain wall, and it has **no column, beam, slab, footing or foundation class** ([CADTransformer anno_config](https://github.com/VITA-Group/CADTransformer/blob/main/config/anno_config.py)). Scores on it have saturated. VecFormer reports PQ 88.4 without priors and 91.1 with priors ([VecFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23395)). The "with priors" variants such as SymPoint-V2 explicitly feed in the CAD layer of each primitive ([SymPoint-V2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01928)), which is exactly the signal you have ruled out. Even inside FloorPlanCAD, wall is one of the hardest classes, at PQ 53.5 for SymPoint ([SymPoint](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10556)).

ArchCAD-400K (NeurIPS 2025) is the only large dataset with **columns, beams, holes, slabs and foundations**. It has 413,062 chunks from 5,538 mostly non-residential drawings, and its DPSS baseline reaches only PQ 70.6 overall ([ArchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22346)). Its code is academic-licensed. Only a 40K subset is released through a gated Hugging Face request ([ArchCAD GitHub](https://github.com/ArchiAI-LAB/ArchCAD)). **No official trained checkpoint exists**: a Hugging Face repo rumoured to hold DPSS weights contains no weight file ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mohansshf/dpss-archcad)), and a community fork warns that without a checkpoint the demo gives "PQ ≈ 0" ([ArchCAD-gpu](https://github.com/Manavbangotra/ArchCAD-gpu)). No published study tests any FloorPlanCAD- or ArchCAD-trained model on European structural or foundation plans. The one practitioner report on cross-country transfer found that the mismatch "lives in sheet context, not in the symbols". It also had to retrain on tiles auto-labelled from CAD layer names ([ArchCAD-gpu taxonomy.py](https://github.com/Manavbangotra/ArchCAD-gpu/blob/master/dataset/taxonomy.py)), which is a dependency you cannot accept.

Raster and open-vocabulary routes are weaker still. Public raster datasets (CubiCasa5K, MLSTRUCT-FP, CVC-FP) label walls and sometimes columns. None of them labels footings, rafts or grade beams. Wall IoU of 0.8 to 0.9 holds only within a dataset ([arXiv 2408.01526](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01526v1)). Zero-shot Grounding DINO on CubiCasa5K scored **mAP@0.5 ≈ 0.0**, with boxes 100 to 330 times too large, while an in-domain YOLOv8s reached 67.1 ([heypaprika/gdino](https://github.com/heypaprika/gdino)). Even trained detectors localise loosely: on CVC-FP, mAP@0.5 is 0.82 but mAP@0.5:0.95 is only 0.48 ([FloorYOLO](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1911589/full)). That is too loose for quantity takeoff when you already hold exact vector coordinates. The only raster technique that fits your no-label constraint is **exemplar prompting**: "find more like this one". SAM 3 supports it with open weights and negative exemplars ([SAM 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)). T-Rex2 does the same, but only through an API ([IDEA-Research/T-Rex](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/T-Rex)). Neither has published numbers on technical drawings.

Frontier VLMs read text well but do not count or localise reliably. On AECV-Bench, text extraction reaches about 95%. Counting does not: the best model counts doors correctly only **39%** of the time and windows 34% ([AECFoundry](https://www.aecfoundry.com/blog/can-ai-really-read-your-building-plans-aecv-bench-gets-a-major-upgrade)). On real scanned reinforced-concrete blueprints, single-MLLM zero-shot beam extraction scored **F1 0.301**. A fixed pipeline scored 0.820, and a constraint-driven agent scored 0.994 ([BlueprintAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07362)). Whole-sheet prompting is also physically lossy: APIs downscale an A0 sheet to roughly 55 to 80 dpi ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/image-token-cost-vlm/)).

Commercial tools confirm the pattern. The mainstream AI takeoff products (Togal, Kreo, Bluebeam Max, Civils.ai) centre on rooms, walls, openings and **example-based symbol search** ([Bluebeam](https://press.bluebeam.com/2026/05/bluebeam-max-launches-globally-bringing-ai-powered-productivity-to-aec-teams-everywhere/); [Kreo API](https://www.kreo.net/features/api)). Kreo's public API, the only documented detection API found, lists no structural members. The explicit French gros-œuvre claim comes from Planaliz: poteaux, poutres, dalles, longrines, pieux and fondations, from vector PDF or DWG, with IFC export. It publishes no accuracy figures and does not disclose its method ([Planaliz](https://planaliz.fr/logiciel-metres-BTP-automatiques)). Beam AI covers structural scopes, but as an AI-plus-human service with a 24 to 72 hour turnaround ([Beam AI](https://www.ibeam.ai/general-contractor-software)). Autodesk's own AutoCAD Smart Blocks sends *vector geometry* to an ML service that groups similar shapes into block candidates ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/view/ACD/2026/ENU/?guid=GUID-7D3E7065-AD01-4720-B5DF-95971BAEFA9A)). A major vendor has therefore made the same choice recommended here: vector candidates first, semantics second.

| Route | Structural classes available? | Needs your labels? | Box precision on vector PDFs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vector PSS (SymPoint, VecFormer, DPSS) | FloorPlanCAD: walls only. ArchCAD: yes, but no weights and academic licence | Yes (fine-tune) | Exact (primitive-level) | Future option after pseudo-labelling |
| Raster detectors (YOLO, RF-DETR) | No public footing/raft data | Yes | Loose (mAP@.5:.95 ≈ 0.48) | Raster fallback only, trained on pseudo-labels |

| Zero-shot VLM on whole sheet | Nominally | No | Poor; counting ~40% | Reject for detection |
| VLM on candidate crops (set-of-marks) | Any | No | Exact (geometry from vectors) | Optional verifier |
| Deterministic geometry and rules | Any you encode | No | Exact | **Core** |

## The drawing is its own training set

With learned models out of reach, the credible alternative is to exploit regularities that every structural drawing office shares, whatever its layer names and colour conventions. The closest published system is a 2026 training-free pipeline for structural framing plans. It runs in a fixed order: scale, grid, column glyphs, members, symbol grammar, topology. It reports columns at **92.2% recall and 99.7% precision**, beams at 88.6% and 99.0%, and walls at 100% on held-out drawings ([arXiv 2608.17237](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17237)). Its components map almost one-to-one onto your problem. Scale comes from a consensus between dimension text and dimension lines with MAD (median absolute deviation) outlier filtering, as you already do. Grids are dash-dot chains anchored by labelled bubbles, with the **grid family assigned by label content (letters vs numbers) rather than direction**, so rotated wings still work. Stroke weights are classified from drawing-specific histogram percentiles, not absolute widths. Hatched bands between columns become shear walls. Members whose ends rest on no support are rejected. The caveat matters: its benchmark was procedurally generated by the authors' own generator, and they state that it "does not address independently drafted plans". Treat its numbers as a ceiling on clean inputs, not a forecast.

Several cues survive any drafting style if you use them *relatively* within each sheet. **Repetition** comes first. Columns and isolated footings are a handful of congruent closed shapes, repeated many times. Classic training-free symbol spotting canonicalises and hashes vector shape descriptors, then votes, "without prior segmentation or a-priori knowledge" ([Rusiñol & Lladós, GREC 2007](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-88188-9_11)). For construction glyphs, a key of sorted (w, h), vertex count and rectangularity is simpler and more robust than Hu moments. **Containment and nesting** is a stronger cue than shape alone. An isolated footing contains exactly one column-sized polygon. A strip footing contains a narrower wall band on its medial axis. French practice fixes these relations directly: semelles isolées under poteaux, filantes under voiles or rows of columns, longrines spanning between footings and carrying walls, and a radier when footings would exceed about 50% of the footprint ([Wikipédia, Semelle de fondation](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semelle_de_fondation)). **Parallel-pair banding** recovers walls, strips and longrines from line soup. Pair segments in the same angle bucket whose offset falls in a thickness window and whose projections overlap. The literature notes that pairs and walls are not bijective, since consecutive walls can share a segment ([ResearchGate figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-mapping-between-pairs-of-segments-and-walls-is-not-bijective-eg-the-walls-ab-and_fig5_256688203)). Lu et al. applied exactly this "parallel pairs" idea to shear walls and bearing beams in construction structure drawings ([IJDAR 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-006-0029-6)). **Hatching** is detectable purely geometrically: thin, equispaced, parallel strokes whose endpoints lie on a common contour ([Dori/Liu hatching figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/llustration-of-detection-of-a-group-of-hatching-lines_fig6_220509852)). It is a colour-free material cue for voiles and concrete sections.

This reframes two of your weaknesses. **Walls drawn as connected networks** and **strips and beams drawn as parallel line pairs** are not labelling problems. They are band-extraction problems. Merge collinear fragments and dash chains, pair parallels, union the pieces, and split at T, L and X junctions using width jumps along a medial axis. **Rafts with unclosed outlines** are a closure problem. Snap endpoints within tolerance, extend near-miss segments, then run `shapely.ops.polygonize_full`, whose dangles and cut-edges tell you exactly where the outline is open. **Slabs defined by footprint** are a face problem: the slab is the building outline minus the union of other elements, or the faces bounded by beams and walls. Each of these yields a geometric candidate with its own confidence, *independent of whether a label is nearby*. That independence is what stops label errors from propagating.

Two cues are free and underused. **Legends and schedules** first. A legend row pairing a glyph with a word is a zero-cost exemplar, and a footing schedule mapping mark to L × B × H lets geometry verify labels. Autodesk-style repeated-geometry grouping and "find all like this" are the commercial versions of the same idea ([Autodesk Smart Blocks](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/AutoCAD-WhatsNew/files/GUID-25BD5FB7-119A-42A8-B1C1-62BB812A3F4F.htm)). Second, **source format**. AutoCAD PDFs flatten blocks, arcs and hatch semantics into paths. SHX text survives only as "AutoCAD SHX Text" comments or hidden text, controlled by PDFSHX ([Autodesk PDFSHX](https://help.autodesk.com/view/ACD/2024/ENU/?guid=GUID-56EA988C-A1DA-4E85-8765-B3F31A01AB02)). A DXF keeps block INSERTs (repetition for free), true arcs, hatch entities, block attributes (exact mark-to-element links) and MULTILEADER arrow tips. It is readable with ezdxf, and DWG can be read through the ODA converter ([ezdxf odafc](https://ezdxf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/addons/odafc.html)). Ask for DWG or DXF whenever you can. Build the pipeline so that DXF feeds the same candidate stage, with block identity as an additional repetition cue, never as a required one.

## Label confusion is a global matching failure, not a distance failure

Your diagnosis is correct: analysing each layer, or each type, independently hid the confusion. Greedy nearest-shape matching per type cannot see that the shape lying between a P label and its column has already been claimed by a V or S label. It also cannot see that a line of sight crosses another element. The published label-association literature for technical drawings is almost entirely **greedy and per-label**. Kim et al.'s P&ID cascade tests containment (≥99% overlap), then line penetration with matching direction, then axis-aligned ray casting from the text-box edges ([Kim et al., JCDE 2022](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/9/4/1298/6611631)). Rahul et al. use nearest-by-Euclidean-distance with a "not separated from the pipeline" condition, and reached only **64%** on pipeline codes ([arXiv 1901.11383](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.11383)). Microsoft's Shapely-proximity approach reports about **10% error in crowded regions** ([Microsoft ISE](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/engineering-document-pid-digitization/)). Those are exactly the failures you see.

The strongest training-free precedent comes from cartography. Budig, van Dijk and Wolff match labels to markers on historical maps with a **global minimum-cost matching** that uses distance and surrounding elements. It solves thousands of elements in seconds and matches **99%** of labels correctly on maps with over 4,000 markers. It adds a sensitivity analysis that surfaces only the ambiguous matches for human review ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2677068.2677073); [project page](http://historical-maps.github.io/)). It transfers directly to drawings. Build one cost matrix over **all** parsed labels and **all** candidates, of every type, and solve it once. In practice use min-cost flow (networkx or OR-Tools), not plain Hungarian. Flow lets one label cover several elements when the text says so ("×4", "TYP", repeated marks). It lets an entity accept identical duplicated text. It also provides dummy "unlabelled element" and "orphan label" arcs with penalties, so that drafting errors abstain instead of forcing a wrong match.

The cost for a label–candidate pair should combine five terms. The first is **type compatibility**: a hard block when the label grammar (P, V, VP, S/SC, SF, LG, Radier) and the candidate's geometric class disagree. The second is **gap distance**, measured from label box to nearest point on the shape (not centroid) and normalised by text height. The third is a **visibility penalty**: the count of other candidates' boundaries crossed by the connecting segment, taking the minimum over a few rays. This is the published "not separated" and "first hit" rule made soft. The fourth is **orientation agreement** for elongated elements. The fifth is **catalogue deviation**, the gap between the measured size and the size the catalogue implies for that mark. Explicit links override all of this and are resolved before the solve: leader lines ending on a shape, text inside a closed shape, text penetrated by a band's axis. That staging mirrors the Kim et al. cascade. Constraints that are not flow constraints need a small CP-SAT or ILP layer. Examples are "all elements carrying mark S3 share one size" and "P sizes are per building". Alternatively, alternate an assignment step with a re-estimation step. The E-step solves the assignment. The M-step re-fits the per-drawing priors from confident matches: preferred label offset and side per type, text height per type, and the per-building and plan-wide size catalogue. Two to five iterations are enough. No drawing paper has published this EM loop, so it is a design proposal built from standard parts. Every component is well-understood, and each confidence comes out as a Budig-style margin: the global cost increase when the chosen pair is forbidden.

This also fixes the **style voting** step. At present it learns styles near labels, keyed partly on layer and colour. Key it instead on a **geometric signature**: closure, fill present or absent (ignoring hue), stroke-weight percentile, dash pattern, and canonical size. Vote only with high-margin matches from the previous iteration. A label on the wrong layer or in the wrong colour then stops mattering, because neither is part of the signature. An outline on an unrelated layer still joins its repetition cluster because its geometry matches.

## The architecture to adopt, and the order to build it

The target is a five-stage pipeline in which every stage is layer-agnostic. Layers and colours, when present, can enter only as weak tie-breaking features that the pipeline measures per drawing.

**Stage 0: ingest and normalise.** Accept DXF or DWG where available (ezdxf, ODA converter), otherwise vector PDF. Extract primitives with stroke width, dash array, fill-present flag and sequence order. Recover text from TrueType spans and from SHX comments or hidden text. Merge collinear fragments and dash chains, sample Béziers or fit circles, and snap endpoints. Detect and mask the title block, tables and legends. Parse legends and schedules into exemplars and a mark-to-dimension table. One licensing flag: PyMuPDF is dual AGPL/commercial ([PyMuPDF docs](https://github.com/pymupdf/PyMuPDF/blob/main/docs/page.rst)), and one public PDF-to-CAD parser deliberately used pikepdf to avoid AGPL ([parse_pdf_plans.py](https://github.com/Manavbangotra/ArchCAD-gpu/blob/master/dataset/parse_pdf_plans.py)). Settle this before shipping.

**Stage 1: frame of reference.** Keep your scale calibration unchanged; it already matches the published state of the art. Add grid detection: dash-dot chains plus bubbles, families by label content, and an intersection lattice. Add building segmentation from the grid families and footprint clusters, since your P and V catalogues are per building.

**Stage 2: candidate generation, with no labels involved.** Produce closed-shape candidates with signatures, and repetition clusters by hashing plus DBSCAN. Produce nesting relations (shape in shape, concentric). Produce bands from parallel pairs and medial axes, split at junctions. Produce hatch and stipple regions, closed faces from polygonize, with open-outline diagnostics, and footprint-derived slab and raft faces. Each candidate carries a class-likelihood vector computed from geometry and topology alone. Examples: "compact, repeated, on a grid node, filled → column-like"; "contains one column-like shape, 0.8 to 4 m → isolated footing-like"; "band 0.2 to 0.6 m ending on footings at both ends, not on a wider band → longrine-like".

**Stage 3: joint interpretation.** Parse labels with a grammar. Detect the sheet language first, because "S" means semelle in French and slab in English. Run the global min-cost flow with the five cost terms and the explicit-link pre-pass. Then run a small CP-SAT pass over the relation graph for structural consistency: every band is exactly one of wall, strip, longrine or other; an isolated footing contains at least one column; a strip carries a wall or a row of columns; a raft covers most columns; and each type mark has one size (per building for P and V, plan-wide for S, SC and SF). Iterate the EM loop. Unlabelled candidates inherit a type and mark from catalogue uniqueness *and* repetition-cluster membership, which extends your current uniqueness rule. Every inferred label is flagged as inferred.

**Stage 4: verification and output.** Output oriented polygons, axis-aligned boxes and quantities from the vector geometry. Attach a confidence margin and QA flags to each element: orphan label, unlabelled element, low margin, catalogue outlier, open outline, band with no support. Optionally send only the flagged candidates to a VLM using set-of-marks crops. The VLM may answer only by candidate ID and choose from a closed set of edits, and each edit must pass a geometric admission test. The 2608.17237 system uses this "fail-closed" pattern, with 12 typed operations ([arXiv 2608.17237](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17237)). BlueprintAgent found constraints work best "as triggers for entity-level targeted revisits rather than as post-hoc output filters" ([arXiv 2609.07362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07362)). Crops keep full resolution, and the cost is cents per sheet ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/image-token-cost-vlm/)).

| Order | Change | Keep / add | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build an evaluation set: 10–20 real plans from several offices, outputs hand-verified, plus a procedural foundation-plan generator for regression | Add | Without it, no change can be measured. The generator gives exact ground truth, as the synthetic benchmark in 2608.17237 does |
| 2 | Replace per-layer greedy matching with a global min-cost flow over all labels and candidates, with type compatibility, visibility and dummies | Add; keep the label grammar and distance features | Directly targets your most damaging error (cross-type confusion) |
| 3 | Re-key style learning on geometric signatures; drop layer and colour as keys | Modify | Removes the dependency on wrong or inconsistent layers and colours |
| 4 | Repetition clustering, nesting and containment | Add | Independent evidence for columns and footings; lets unlabelled elements be found |
| 5 | Band extraction (parallel pairs, junction splitting, dash merging) and hatch detection | Add | Walls, strips and longrines as first-class candidates |
| 6 | Grid detection and building segmentation | Add | Anchors columns; defines the per-building catalogue scope |
| 7 | Catalogue as a soft or hard constraint inside the EM loop; label recovery via catalogue plus clusters | Keep and extend | Your catalogue idea, made joint and iterative |
| 8 | Closure repair, face extraction for raft and slab, topology CSP | Add | Rafts and slabs, plus structural consistency |
| 9 | DXF/DWG ingestion path | Add | Exact blocks, attributes and leaders when available |
| 10 | Optional VLM verifier on flagged crops; optional SAM 3 exemplar path for raster sheets | Add last | Improves the tail; never the backbone |

Learning comes in only after this works. Once the rule pipeline runs on many real sheets, keep only instances that satisfy several independent cues: repetition, containment, grid alignment and label agreement. Use them as pseudo-labels to train a primitive-level model, VecFormer-style or GAT-style, or a tile detector for raster inputs. Readmit its predictions only through the same guards. Self-training works when pseudo-labels are filtered for reliability and grown progressively ([Self-Training survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12040)). Synthetic pre-training plus a few hundred pseudo-labelled real sheets is the realistic data budget. With only 98 real training images, FloorYOLO reached mAP@0.5 of 0.82 on CVC-FP ([FloorYOLO](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1911589/full)). Two licence notes: permissive detectors such as YOLOX (Apache-2.0) avoid YOLOv8's AGPL ([heypaprika/gdino](https://github.com/heypaprika/gdino)), and FloorPlanCAD and CubiCasa5K are non-commercial.

For **raster sheets**, run this order: binarise, tile, and vectorise, or run exemplar spotting with SAM 3 or normalised cross-correlation (seeded by legend glyphs or by high-confidence detections on the same sheet). Then snap the results to the vectorised lines and send them into the same Stage 3. OCR replaces the SHX annotations as the text source. Expect lower quality, and route more of the output to review.

## Realistic accuracy and where it will still break

No published benchmark covers foundation plans. The expectations below are therefore engineering judgement, anchored to the nearest evidence: 92–99% for columns and beams on clean synthetic framing plans ([arXiv 2608.17237](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17237)); 99% for global label matching on noisy maps ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2677068.2677073)); 64–97% for greedy association in P&IDs ([arXiv 1901.11383](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.11383)); and 0.30 F1 for zero-shot MLLM extraction ([arXiv 2609.07362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07362)).

| Element | Clean vector PDF, labelled drawing (estimate) | Main residual failure modes |
|---|---|---|
| Columns / poteaux | ~92–98% recall, ≥98% precision | Off-grid or eccentric columns; upper-floor columns shown dashed on foundation plans; columns merged into voiles |
| Isolated and combined footings (S, SC) | ~90–97% recall, high precision | Combined footings containing two columns misread as two S; stepped footings drawn as nested outlines |
| Strip footings (SF) | ~80–90% on length | Junction splitting; strips hidden under walls drawn dashed; discontinuous outlines |
| Voiles (shear and retaining walls) | ~85–95% on length | Wall networks where thickness changes mid-run; openings; hatch absent |
| Longrines (LG) | ~80–90% | Confusion with strips of similar width; ends not visibly on supports |
| Radier / slab | Area within a few % when the outline closes; otherwise flagged | Unclosed outlines; footprint-defined slabs with ambiguous edges; thickened zones |
| Label-to-element association | ~95–99% with global assignment and QA flags | Labels placed far away with no leader; one label for a series; genuinely wrong labels |
| Raster sheets | Substantially lower across the board | Vectorisation noise, OCR errors, merged glyphs |

The failure modes that no architecture removes are **genuine drafting errors**: a label naming the wrong type, a size that contradicts the schedule, an element simply not drawn. The pipeline's job there is to detect the contradiction and flag it, not to guess. The pipeline also degrades predictably when an office's conventions break its assumptions. Examples are sheets with no grid (small residential), line weights flattened by the exporter, text vectorised with no SHX annotations, or notation never seen before. Each new convention still needs a rule, which is the stated limitation of the best training-free system ([arXiv 2608.17237](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17237)). Finally, the ranges above are unvalidated until you measure them on your own verified set. That is why the evaluation set is step one, not an afterthought.

## Conclusion

The central shift is from *label-driven detection* to *evidence fusion with labels as one voter*. Your current pipeline treats a label as the anchor that makes a shape an element. That is why drafting errors in labels, layers and colours cascade into detection errors. Once geometry, repetition, containment, grid and topology each produce candidates and class likelihoods independently, and a single global assignment reconciles them with the labels and the catalogue, a wrong label becomes a detectable *disagreement* instead of a silent error. The same structure turns every ambiguous case into a quantified margin, so human review scales with a drawing's difficulty, not its size.

Strategically, the research points to a durable advantage in owning the deterministic core and treating models as replaceable plug-ins. Structural classes are under-served both by public data and by the big takeoff vendors. The pipeline's own high-confidence outputs are the cheapest route to the labelled corpus that learned vector models would need. A VLM earns its place only as a bounded verifier on crops, and a trained detector only as a student of the rules. Asking clients for DWG or DXF has the highest return of any single change, because it turns repetition and mark association from inference into lookup.
